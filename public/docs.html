OVERVIEW

What is Hedgora?

Hedgora is an AI travel companion. It finds places worth visiting around you, plans your time, and lets you pay locals in USDC on Arc, Circle’s network where USDC is real money, but only when you approve.

⌖Explore like a local

Real food spots, sights and places to stay near you, with maps and directions.

◈Non-custodial wallet

Your wallet is created and secured by Circle. Hedgora never holds your keys.

◇You always approve

Hedgora only prepares payments. Nothing is sent until you confirm it.

QUICK START

Connect a wallet in a minute

  1. Open Hedgora and click Get Start in the top-right corner.
  2. Choose Continue with Google and sign in. You go to Google and come straight back to Hedgora.
  3. Circle creates an Arc wallet for you. If Circle’s secure window appears, confirm it to finish.
  4. When the top-right button shows an address like 0x…, you’re ready. Signing in with the same Google account always reopens the same wallet.
  5. Send USDC on Arc to your wallet address to fund it. USDC also pays the network fee on Arc, so keep a little spare.
This is real money. USDC on Arc has real value, payments cannot be undone, and nobody can reverse a payment sent to the wrong address.
Keep your Google account safe: your wallet is tied to it, and neither Hedgora nor Circle can recover the wallet if you lose access.
FEATURES

What you can do with Hedgora

✦

Ask Hedgora AI

Type a question in Where should we go next? or tap a quick prompt such as Local food or Plan my afternoon. DeepSeek answers using your location, local time, weather and the real places around you.

▣

Discover nearby

Up to six real places around you from OpenStreetMap: sights, food, a café and a place to stay, with real distances. Landmarks show their own photo from Wikimedia Commons or Openverse when one matches; other places show an Area photo taken nearby, labeled with how far away. Use Update to refresh, Directions for a route and Pay to open the Payments page. See all on Explore opens the full list.

⌕

Explore page

Every named place Hedgora finds near you on OpenStreetMap, up to 25 of each kind: sights within 10 km, restaurants and cafés within 2 km, places to stay within 3 km. Filter by kind or name, see them on the map, and get directions on foot, by car, by bike or by motorbike. Tap Details for today’s opening hours, whether it’s open now, the price and photos from Google Maps, when Google lists them. For sights, Details also quotes ticket prices that visitors mention in Google reviews, and lists bookable tours and tickets from Viator.

⌖

Map & directions

Click View map to find restaurants, hotels and attractions within 5 km. Pick one to see the distance and travel time by car, on foot, by bike or by motorbike. In Vietnam, car and motorbike routes come from VIETMAP; walking, cycling and routes elsewhere come from OSRM.

☀

Weather & sunset

Current temperature and conditions for your location, plus the time of the next sunset (or sunrise after dark) and how long until it. Both refresh when you update your location.

◷

Smart itinerary

Hedgora AI plans your next few hours from real places around you, fitted to the local time and weather. Every stop shows the real distance and a Directions button; New plan asks for another. Open in Trips shows the same plan on a map.

⇢

Trips page

Your plan with numbered stops on a map and the route through them, with the travel time of each leg from VIETMAP (car and motorbike in Vietnam) or OSRM. Switch to My saved places to plan with places you saved within 20 km. Your saved places are listed below it.

◎

Your location

Click Update next to “Your location”, or Use my location on the map. Until you share it, Hedgora uses central Vũng Tàu. In Vietnam the name shows your ward and district or city from VIETMAP, for example “Phường Thắng Tam, Thành Phố Vũng Tàu”.

♡

Saved places

Tap ♡ on a place card, or Save on Explore and Trips, to save it. Saved places on the homepage and the Trips page list them with Pay, Remove and a way to see them on the map. They are stored in this browser only and don’t sync to other devices.

PAYMENTS & SECURITY

Every payment needs your approval

The Payments page sends and receives USDC on Arc with your Circle wallet.

  1. Connect your wallet with Google. The page shows your USDC balance (with a VND estimate) and your address. Send USDC on Arc to that address to top it up; the balance refreshes when you come back.
  2. Enter or paste the recipient’s address, the amount and an optional note, then click Review payment. Hedgora checks the address (format, EIP-55 checksum, burn addresses), asks Circle for the fee and total, and warns you if this is your first payment to that address or if the amount is over 100 USDC.
  3. Click Confirm in Circle and approve in Circle’s secure window. If you close that window, nothing is sent. When the transfer is complete, you get a receipt you can copy, share (on devices that support sharing) or open on Arc Explorer.
  4. To get paid, use Get paid with a QR code: add an amount and note if you like. The payer scans the code with their phone camera and the Payments page opens with your address filled in.
  5. Recent transactions lists what you sent and received, with All, Sent and Received filters. Tap a payment for its details, and use Load more for older ones. Payments still in progress update on their own.
Note: the Pay button on a place card opens the Payments page with the place name as the note. OpenStreetMap places don’t have wallets, so you need the merchant’s Hedgora QR code or wallet address.

How Hedgora keeps you safe

  • Non-custodial wallet. Circle creates and secures your wallet. Hedgora cannot access your keys.
  • Approvals happen in Circle’s window. Sensitive steps run in Circle’s secure interface, not on Hedgora’s pages.
  • Sign in with Google. Hedgora never sees your Google password.
  • API keys stay on the server. The Circle and DeepSeek keys live only on the backend and are never sent to the browser.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does Hedgora use real money?

Yes. Hedgora runs on Arc, Circle’s network, and the USDC there is real money. A payment is final once you approve it: it cannot be cancelled or refunded by Hedgora or by Circle, so check the recipient before you confirm.

How do I put USDC in my wallet?

Send USDC on Arc to your Hedgora wallet address, which the Payments page shows with a QR code. USDC also pays the network fee on Arc, so keep a little spare for fees.

Why sign in with Google?

Circle uses your Google account to verify you and link the wallet to you. You don’t need another password, and the same Google account always opens the same wallet.

I signed in but don’t see my wallet address

A new wallet can take a few seconds to appear. Wait a moment, then click Get Start to sign in again. If it still fails, the message under Continue with Google explains why.

Google sign-in says it was cancelled or failed

This usually means the Google window was closed early, so try again.

What if I lose access to my Google account?

Your wallet is tied to the Google account you sign in with, and recovery depends entirely on signing in with it again. Neither Hedgora nor Circle can access your keys or recover the wallet for you, so keep that Google account secure.

Do I have to share my location?

No. Hedgora uses central Vũng Tàu until you tap Update or Use my location. When you share it, your location is used for nearby places, weather, sunset time and photos, and it is sent to the services that provide them (Open-Meteo, Wikimedia Commons, Openverse, which gets the names of sights and your city, and on the map OpenStreetMap, Overpass and the OSRM routing servers). For nearby places, your location rounded to about 500 m goes through Hedgora’s server to Photon or Overpass. In Vietnam, your coordinates and the places you route to also go through Hedgora’s server to VIETMAP for the location name and car or motorbike routes. When you ask Hedgora AI or get a plan, your location name and nearby places go through Hedgora’s server to DeepSeek; Ask Hedgora also sends your coordinates. When you tap Details on a place, its name and coordinates and your location name go through Hedgora’s server to SerpApi, which looks the place up on Google Maps. For sights, Hedgora’s server also asks Viator for tours in the nearest Viator destination; Viator gets only that destination, not your coordinates. Hedgora’s server does not save them. Your browser remembers a location you share for that tab only, so Explore and Trips use it too.

DEVELOPERS

Architecture & running locally

Hedgora is a static frontend plus a small backend that holds the API keys.

  • Frontend (public/): index.html, explore.html, trips.html, payments.html, docs.html, circle-config.js, hedgora-places.js (places, photos, saved places, map and routing shared by Explore and Trips; the homepage uses its photos), and circle-wallet-client.js, the Circle Web SDK bundle built with Vite (the SDK itself loads lazily from circle-index.js).
  • Backend (server.js): Hono on Node. It holds CIRCLE_API_KEY, DEEPSEEK_API_KEY, VIETMAP_API_KEY and SERPAPI_API_KEY and calls the Circle User-Controlled Wallets API, the DeepSeek API, the VIETMAP reverse geocoding and routing APIs, and SerpApi’s Google Maps APIs.

Sign-in flow

  1. The browser gets a deviceId from the Circle Web SDK and calls POST /api/social/token for a deviceToken and deviceEncryptionKey.
  2. The SDK redirects to Google, which returns an id_token to the page in the URL.
  3. The SDK verifies the token with Circle and receives a userToken and encryptionKey.
  4. POST /api/user/initialize creates an SCA wallet on the configured network (ARC by default) and returns a challengeId for the SDK to run, or null if the user already has a wallet.
  5. POST /api/wallets/list returns the wallet address to display.

Run locally

npm install
cp .env.example .env   # fill in CIRCLE_API_KEY, DEEPSEEK_API_KEY, VIETMAP_API_KEY, SERPAPI_API_KEY, VIATOR_API_KEY
npm run build          # builds public/circle-wallet-client.js
npm start              # site + API: http://localhost:5173

External services

  • Google Cloud Console: create an OAuth Client ID of type Web application and add the site’s origin to both Authorized JavaScript origins and Authorized redirect URIs.
  • Circle Console: Configurator → Authentication Methods → Social Logins → Google, then paste that Client ID.
  • Photon and Overpass (no key): GET /api/places/nearby runs the four nearby searches on Photon, each giving up after 5 seconds; if all fail, it asks two public Overpass servers at once and takes the first answer (8 seconds at most). A complete answer is cached per spot on the server for 12 hours and on Vercel’s CDN (s-maxage=43200, stale for up to a week while it refreshes). Pages round the spot to about 500 m, keep the answer in the browser for 6 hours (shown at once for up to a week while a fresh copy loads), and only call Photon directly if the server can’t be reached.
  • Wikimedia Commons (no key): a sight’s own photo is searched by name within 1 km of it. Area photos come from one geosearch per cell of about 550 m, which returns the 50 geotagged photos nearest the cell for every place in it; each scene is used once per page and maps or logos are skipped. Requests run one at a time with an Api-User-Agent header and pause for a minute after a 429.
  • Openverse (no key): when Commons has no photo of a sight, Openverse is searched for the sight’s name and city, keeping only licenses that allow commercial use and results whose title or tags name the city and at least half of the sight’s distinctive words. Anonymous use allows 20 searches a minute and 200 a day per visitor, so searches run one at a time, at most 18 in any minute, and pause after a 429.
  • Photo answers, including “no photo”, are cached in the browser for 7 days. Each photo shows its author and license and links to its own page.
  • SerpApi (SERPAPI_API_KEY): each Details tap is one Google Maps search, in Vietnamese so names match OpenStreetMap, and More photos is one more. The free plan has 250 searches a month, so answers (including “not on Google Maps”) are cached in the browser for 3 days (photos for 7) and on each server instance for a day. For sights, the ticket prices come from the listing’s reviews; a search that returns a list has none, so that costs one more search.
  • Viator (VIATOR_API_KEY, free Basic-access affiliate): /destinations finds the nearest destination within 60 km and /products/search returns up to 100 of its products priced in VND (two pages of 50), minus cruise shore excursions and airport transfers; both are cached for a day on each server instance and in the browser. Product links are Viator’s affiliate productUrl, and the page says Hedgora may earn a commission.

Deploy to Vercel

  1. Import the GitHub repo into Vercel. vercel.json already selects the Hono framework, runs npm run build and enables clean URLs (/explore, /trips, /payments, /docs).
  2. In Settings → Environment Variables, add CIRCLE_API_KEY, DEEPSEEK_API_KEY, VIETMAP_API_KEY, SERPAPI_API_KEY and (optional) VIATOR_API_KEY, then redeploy.
  3. In Google Cloud Console, add your domain (for this site, https://www.hedgora.xyz) to both Authorized JavaScript origins and Authorized redirect URIs.
  • Vercel serves public/ from its CDN, and server.js runs as a Vercel Function for every /api/* path and for the homepage (/).
  • The API sends no CORS headers, so web pages on other domains can’t call it from a browser. Scripts outside a browser still can, which is why the AI endpoints are rate-limited.
  • Never put an API key in circle-config.js or any other file in public/.
